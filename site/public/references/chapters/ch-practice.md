# Chapter 7 · Train your eye, design in the real material

> Evidence boundary: this is a learning guide. Its demos and product examples are illustrations written for the book, not verified product documentation, so check a product’s own docs before citing how it behaves. Tools named in it are 2026 examples, not endorsements.

Taste is a skill you train. You build it by looking closely at real products, rebuilding the details that work, and naming why they work. As a design engineer, you also have an edge: you can design in the real material, where timing, real data and every state are real from the first minute. The entries below turn both into habits: look closely, try the risky part in code, and write down why you chose what you chose.

## Entries

- [Train your eye](../entries/good-train-your-eye.md): You train taste by looking closely at good work.
- [Real material](../entries/good-real-material.md): Prototyping in the real material means building the risky parts of a design in code, early.
- [Design rationale](../entries/good-design-rationale.md): Design rationale is the reasoning behind a design decision, written down.
