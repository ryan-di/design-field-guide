# Chapter 4 · Treat the words as the interface

Most of an interface is words. People read the label on a button, the title of a dialog and the first line of an error long before they notice the layout. Each of those words sets what people expect to happen next, so writing them is design work. The entries below share one test: can someone act on the words alone?

## Entries

- [Labels](../entries/good-labels.md): A button label should say what happens when people press it: a verb, and the thing it acts on.
- [Error messages](../entries/good-error-copy.md): A good error message says what happened, why if you know, and what to do next, in plain words that don’t blame anyone.
- [Plain language](../entries/good-plain-language.md): Plain language means the words people already use, in short sentences, with the main point first and one name for each concept.
