> Evidence boundary: this is a learning guide. Its demos and product examples are illustrations written for the book, not verified product documentation, so check a product’s own docs before citing how it behaves. Tools named in it are 2026 examples, not endorsements.

The four parts meet in [the loop](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-loop). You frame the problem and set success criteria with Part 1, explore and build with the patterns and components in Parts 2 and 3, and check the result with the critique and review from Part 1 again. What you learn then starts the next round.

To start, take a ticket you already have. Before you open a design tool or an editor, [frame the problem](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-problem-framing): who has it, what they’re trying to do, what stops them, and how you’ll know it’s better. Then, before it ships, go through [Before you ship, ask these](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#ch-good-checklist).
