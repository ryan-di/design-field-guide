Watching people use a design is the quickest way to see where it fails. Give a few real people a task, stay quiet while they try it, and note where they stall. Five people usually show the problems that matter, because the big ones come up again and again.

Analytics are different: they show where many people drop off, but not why. A session shows the why, one person at a time. An agent’s review can’t stand in for one: it predicts what people might do, and a session shows what they do.

## Rules

Four rules keep these sessions useful.

### 1. Watch what people do

https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-quick-sessions-rule-1

Ask people whether they’d use something, and most will say yes, because they want to be kind and can’t know what they’ll do later. What they do with a real task is better evidence, so give them the task and watch where they go.

### 2. Describe the goal, not the feature

https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-quick-sessions-rule-2

If the task names the feature, people look for that word on screen and find it, so you learn that they can read, not that they’d find their way. Describe what they want to get done, in their own words, and you see where they look first.

### 3. Stay quiet when they get stuck

https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-quick-sessions-rule-3

Silence feels awkward, so you’ll want to help as soon as someone stalls. But the stall is the finding, and if you help, you never learn why it happened. Note where it happened and wait. Ask “What did you expect?” at the end, and only step in if they want to give up.

### 4. Fix what repeats

https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-quick-sessions-rule-4

One person’s trouble may be theirs alone. If you change the design after each session, every person sees a different version, and you can’t tell which problems repeat. Run all the sessions on the same version and tally what went wrong, then fix what more than one person hit.

## In real products

Notice that each one watches what people do, not what they say, and looks for problems that repeat.

- GV’s design sprint: on the last day, five customers each try the prototype in a one-on-one interview while the team watches from another room. Notes go on a grid, one column per person, so problems that repeat stand out. `eff-quick-sessions-example-01`
- UserTesting: you write the tasks, and people record their screen and voice while they try them on their own. You watch the recordings and clip the moments where they get stuck. `eff-quick-sessions-example-02`
- Maze: you give testers a task on a clickable prototype and mark the path you expect. It shows how many finished, where they left that path, and a heatmap of clicks on each screen. `eff-quick-sessions-example-03`
- FullStory: replays real people’s sessions and flags rage clicks, many quick clicks on one spot. Searching for them across sessions shows which stuck point hits many people, not just one. `eff-quick-sessions-example-04`
