# Glossary

Design Field Guide v35 (2026-11) · Back matter
Live: https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#ch-glossary
Cite as: Design Field Guide v35, #ch-glossary

> Evidence boundary: this is a learning guide. Its demos and product examples are illustrations written for the book, not verified product documentation, so check a product’s own docs before citing how it behaves. Tools named in it are 2026 examples, not endorsements.

Terms this book uses in a specific sense, each with the entry that explains it.

- **A/B test**: A test where each group of people gets a different version, so you can compare what they do. It’s judged on one main number agreed before the test, plus a [guardrail](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#gl-guardrail). [Measure against the brief](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-measure)
- **Artifact**: Something small a stage of the work leaves for others to read or try, such as a brief, a decision or a plan. In Part 4 it is often an artifact page: one shared web page an agent made. [Follow one problem through](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-worked-example)
- **Attention, lifetime and control**: The three things every component promises: how much it interrupts, how long it stays, and what people can do with it. [Read every component as a promise](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#ch-start)
- **Axis**: One dimension with two ends that options vary along, such as how loud a message is, from silent to blocking. Two axes make a grid, and each cell is a [direction](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#gl-direction). [Vary along named axes](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-axes)
- **Bet**: The guess about what people need that a direction is built around, such as “never interrupt” or “make it fixable in one step”. [Get many directions cheaply](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-explore-wide)
- **Brief**: A short note written before anyone builds: who has the problem, what should change, what’s off limits and how you’ll know it worked. [Write the problem brief](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-brief)
- **Context pack**: What a new teammate would need on their first day: design tokens, components to reuse, copy rules, examples of good screens, repo conventions and the design frames. [Pack the context](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-context-pack)
- **Critique**: Judging work against what it is for: start from the goal, say what serves it and what doesn’t, then suggest a fix. It comes in three kinds: direction for early sketches, flow once the steps exist as rough screens, and polish when the screens are close to done. [Critique against the goal](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-critique)
- **Decision matrix**: A table that scores each direction from 1 to 5 against each criterion in the brief. Each criterion has a [weight](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#gl-weight), so what the problem is about counts more. [Score directions against the brief](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-decision-matrix)
- **Definition of done**: The list of checks a change must pass before you call it finished. Each is something you could see or run, and says who checks it. [Define done so both of you can check it](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-definition-of-done)
- **Design rationale**: The reasoning behind a design decision, written down: the problem, the options you considered, the one you chose and why. [Write down the reasoning](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-design-rationale)
- **Design token**: A shared value with a name, such as a corner radius, a spacing step, a color or a text size. [Design tokens](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-design-tokens)
- **Diff**: The lines a change adds and removes, as a pull request shows them. Skim it for new strings, styles and components. [Call out what people will see](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-ux-calls)
- **Direction**: One clearly different answer to the brief, explored on its own, so you can lay several side by side and choose. [Get many directions cheaply](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-explore-wide)
- **End-to-end spec**: A test that uses the app the way people do, through its screens, such as a Playwright spec. Unit tests check the logic underneath. [Let agents prove their work](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-agent-verification)
- **Feature flag**: A switch that keeps new code turned off for people, so a step can merge before the step that completes it lands. [Plan, then take small steps](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-small-steps)
- **Fidelity**: How close a sketch, mock or prototype is to the real thing, from a sketch to a branch in the app. Each level shows some things and hides others. [See what each fidelity can show](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-heuristic-review-fidelity)
- **Guardrail**: One number that must not get worse, with a limit you set before looking. [Measure against the brief](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-measure)
- **Heuristic review**: A check of a design against a short list of rules of thumb, such as Jakob Nielsen’s ten usability heuristics. It finds likely problems, not proven ones. [Heuristic review](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-heuristic-review)
- **Interruption ladder**: The ways to tell people something, from quietest to loudest: inline message, badge, toast, banner, dialog. Use the lowest step that still does the job. [Read every component as a promise](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#ch-start)
- **Loop**: The stages every change goes through: Problem, Brief, Explore, Decide, Build, Check and Learn. What Learn finds starts the next round. [The loop](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-loop)
- **MCP**: A standard way to plug tools into an agent, such as a connector that lets it read Figma frames. [Pack the context](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-context-pack)
- **Pattern**: A solution shape that has worked many times before, such as undo instead of a confirm. Every pattern has a [trade-off](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#gl-trade-off). [Name the problem before the fix](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#ch-patterns-start)
- **Plan mode**: A way of working where the agent proposes a plan and changes nothing until you agree to it, such as Claude Code’s plan mode. [Plan, then take small steps](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-small-steps)
- **Problem frame**: Who has the problem, what they’re trying to do and what stops them, written down before anyone draws a screen. [Frame the problem](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-problem-framing)
- **Severity**: How bad a usability problem is, from 0 to 4: 0 isn’t a problem after all, 1 is cosmetic, 2 minor, 3 major, where people struggle or lose work, and 4 a catastrophe, where they can’t finish the task. [See the severity scale](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-heuristic-review-severity)
- **Subagent**: A helper agent that works alongside the others, such as one for each direction. [Get many directions cheaply](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-explore-wide)
- **Success criteria**: What you would see or count if a change worked. [Define “good” before building](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#good-success-criteria)
- **Trade-off**: What a pattern costs. Hidden things get forgotten, for example, and experts pay an extra click. [Name the problem before the fix](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#ch-patterns-start)
- **Usability test**: Watching real people use a design to do a task, and noting where they stall. It finds problems that a review can only guess at. [Watch a few people use it](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-quick-sessions)
- **UX call**: A choice people will see in a change, such as the wording, the spacing or which kind of dialog appears. Decide each one and name it in the pull request. [Call out what people will see](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-ux-calls)
- **Weight**: How much a criterion counts in a [decision matrix](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#gl-decision-matrix). [Score directions against the brief](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-decision-matrix)
- **Worktree**: A separate working copy of the code, made with git, so several agents can work at once without clashing. [Get many directions cheaply](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#eff-explore-wide)
