# Chapter 2 · Make the important thing obvious

People scan a screen before they read it: in the first moment they see a few shapes, guess what the screen is for, and look for the thing they came to do. Clarity comes from hierarchy, grouping, rhythm and contrast, long before color or style. The four entries below cover what stands out, what belongs together, what lines up, and what can be read at all.

## Entries

- [Visual hierarchy](../entries/good-visual-hierarchy.md): Visual hierarchy is the order in which people notice things on a screen.
- [Proximity](../entries/good-proximity.md): Proximity means that things close together look like they belong together.
- [Spacing rhythm](../entries/good-spacing-rhythm.md): Spacing rhythm means every gap on a screen comes from a short scale of sizes, such as 4, 8, 16, 24 and 32 pixels, and the edges of things line up.
- [Contrast](../entries/good-contrast.md): Contrast is how clearly text and controls stand apart from what’s behind them.
