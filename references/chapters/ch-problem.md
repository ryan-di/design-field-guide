# Chapter 1 · Get the problem right first

> Evidence boundary: this is a learning guide. Its demos and product examples are illustrations written for the book, not verified product documentation, so check a product’s own docs before citing how it behaves. Tools named in it are 2026 examples, not endorsements.

Most bad design is a good answer to the wrong question: the layout is tidy and the button works, yet it solves something nobody had, or skips what people needed first. The three entries below are the work that comes before any pixels: know what has to hold, frame the problem, and decide how you’ll tell it worked.

## Entries

- [Hierarchy of needs](../entries/good-needs-hierarchy.md): The hierarchy of needs is an order for building a feature.
- [Problem framing](../entries/good-problem-framing.md): Framing the problem means writing down who has it, what they’re trying to do and what stops them, before anyone draws a screen.
- [Success criteria](../entries/good-success-criteria.md): Success criteria say what you would see or count if a change worked.
