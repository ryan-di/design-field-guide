Templates and starter content give people a working example to start from, instead of an empty page. People see what a finished result looks like and change it into their own, so they reach a first result faster and see what the product can do.

Where a [preset](https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#pat-presets) fills in settings, a template gives a whole starting document, with content people are meant to replace. After a template opens, move focus to the first thing to change.

## Ways to do it

There are four common ways to give people a start. They differ in where the start comes from: a gallery of templates, a filled-in example in a new account, people’s own past work, or a few one-click starts on an empty page.

- **Gallery.** Several templates, shown before people start.
- **Sample content.** A new account comes with a filled-in example that teaches by doing.
- **Copy your own.** People start from something they made before.
- **Starters on the page.** A blank page with a few one-click starts on it.

## Rules

Four rules keep templates useful.

### 1. Offer a few different starts

https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#pat-templates-rule-1

If every template works the same way, people take it as the only way and fit their content into it, even when it doesn’t suit them. Offer a few starts that differ in kind, and say in one line what each is good for, so people pick by goal and see the range of what the product can do.

### 2. Keep templates up to date

https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#pat-templates-rule-2

Templates go stale: an old year, renamed features, a chart type that no longer works. A broken template is often someone’s first experience of the product, so own templates like features: give each one an owner, check it with every release, and update or retire it.

### 3. Mark what to change

https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#pat-templates-rule-3

A good example looks finished, so people can’t tell which parts are theirs to change, and the example text they miss ends up in what they send. Mark the parts meant to be edited with words and a border, not color alone, and add one line that names the first thing to do.

### 4. Keep a blank start one click away

https://ryan-di.github.io/design-field-guide/#pat-templates-rule-4

People who know what they want don’t need a template, and if every new file opens a gallery first, they click past it each time. Keep “Blank” as an equal choice next to the templates, one click away.

## In real products

Notice that a blank start stays one click away, and each template already works before you change anything.

- Google Docs: “Blank” is the first tile, in the same row as the templates for a resume or a letter. `pat-templates-example-01`
- Blender: a new file starts from a task, like Sculpting or Video Editing, and General opens with a cube, a camera and a light, so the first render works before you’ve built anything. `pat-templates-example-02`
- Instagram: a Reels template keeps another reel’s cuts and song, and leaves a slot for each of your clips, so it’s clear which parts are yours to fill. `pat-templates-example-03`
